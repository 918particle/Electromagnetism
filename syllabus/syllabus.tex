\begin{abstract}
This course provides a rigorous exploration of classical electrodynamics, including new computational electromagnetism (CEM) content.  The course begins with a review of vector calculus and its physical interpretation in electromagnetism.  Next, the electrostatics of charge distributions using Coulomb's law, Gauss's law, and the electric potential will be presented. The course proceeds to boundary-value problems and solutions to Laplace's equation, including multipole expansions and the method of images.  Building on the introductory content, the course procedes to properties and behavior of electric fields in matter, covering polarization, bound charges, and the electric displacement field.  From electrostatics, the course proceeds to magnetostatics, introducing the Biot–Savart law, Amp\`{e}re's law, vector potential, and the role of magnetization in materials.  New CEM content serves as an introduction to time-dependent electromagnetic fields.  Throughout the course, emphasis is placed on analytical problem-solving through active learning strategies.  Final projects allow students to creatively explore scientific and engineering applications of electrodynamics and CEM.
\end{abstract}

\begin{itemize}

\item To master techniques in mathematical physics and CEM and apply them to theoretical physics and engineering

\end{itemize}

\textit{\textbf{Course Outline}:}
\begin{outline}[enumerate]
\1 \textbf{Vector Analysis, Chapter 1}
\2 Vector algebra
\2 Differential calculus in three dimensions
\2 Integral calculus in three dimensions
\2 Cylindrical and spherical coordinate systems
\2 The Dirac Delta function
\2 Theory of vector fields
\1 \textbf{Electrostatics, Chapter 2}
\2 The electric field
\2 Divergence and curl of electrostatic fields
\2 Electric potential
\2 Work and energy in electrostatics
\2 Conductors
\1 \textbf{Potentials, Chapter 3}
\2 Laplace's equation
\2 The method of images
\2 Separation of variables
\2 Multipole expansion
\1 \textbf{Electric Fields in Matter, Chapter 4}
\2 Polarization
\2 The field of a polarized object
\2 The electric displacement
\2 Linear dielectrics
\1 \textbf{Magnetostatics, Chapter 5}
\2 The Lorentz force law
\2 The Biot-Savart law
\2 The divergence and curl of magnetic fields
\2 Magnetic vector potential
\1 \textbf{Magnetic Fields in Matter, Chapter 6}
\2 Magnetization
\2 The field of a magnetized object
\2 The auxiliary field
\2 Linear and non-linear media
\1 \textbf{Electrodynamics, Chapter 7}
\2 The electromotive force
\2 Electromagnetic induction
\2 Maxwell's equations
\2 The field of a rotating magnet
\1 \textbf{Computational Electromagnetism, CEM}
\2 The finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) technique
\2 Defining boundary conditions in two and three dimensions
\2 Defining radiating sources
\2 Examples and applications
\3 Simple waveguides and coaxial cables
\3 Fiber optic couplers
\3 Radio-frequency (RF) phased arrays
\3 CEM enhanced with machine learning techniques
\3 Introduction to the Naval Engineering Education Consortium (NEEC) research at Whittier Collge and NSWC Corona
\end{outline}
